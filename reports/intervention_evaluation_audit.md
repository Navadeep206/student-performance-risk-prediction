## 1. Executive Summary & Audit Mandate

This audit independently investigated the student-level intervention ranking methodology implemented in Phase 6, specifically the **Peak-Risk Aggregation Rule**:
$$P_s = \max_{a} \hat{p}_{s, a}$$

### Core Audit Conclusion:
**The Peak-Risk Aggregation rule ($P_s = \max_a \hat{p}_{s, a}$) incorporates predictions from future assessment opportunities across disparate calendar dates (spanning up to 9 months). It represents a retrospective academic post-mortem rather than a prospectively actionable advisor decision list.**

1. **Future Prediction Pooling:** In Phase 6, $P_s$ was derived by scanning all assessments taken by student $s$ across the entire academic year (days 12 to 261). Among the top 10% highest-risk students, **74.87% of peak risk probabilities were generated by assessments scheduled after Day 120** (late in the semester).
2. **Retrospective Label Inflation:** The student-level label was defined retrospectively as $Y_s = \max_a y_{s, a}$ (student failed *at least one* assessment across their entire course tenure). This inflated the effective positive base rate from **8.56%** (snapshot failure rate) to **33.49%** (1 in 3 students fail at least once).
3. **Artifact of 96.27% Precision:** The 96.27% precision at top 10% capacity is an artifact of this retrospective label matching: **33.5% of students in the top 10% did not fail the specific assessment that generated their peak probability**—they were credited as true positives solely because they failed some *other* assessment earlier or later in the term.
4. **Leakage-Free Prospective Alternatives Implemented:**
   - **Alternative A (First-Assessment Cohort Milestone):** Evaluates every student strictly 7 days before their first course assessment ($N=23,351$). Reaching the top 10% captures **38.12% of failing students** with **41.03% precision** ($3.8\times$ baseline prevalence of 10.76%).
   - **Alternative B (Common Calendar Cutoff at Day 30):** Evaluates every active student for their next upcoming assessment scheduled after Day 30 ($N=21,719$). Reaching the top 10% captures **41.11% of failing students** with **31.72% precision** ($4.1\times$ baseline prevalence of 7.72%).

### 2.1 Verification 1: Definition of Student-Level Positive Label
- **Formula:** $Y_s = \max_{a} y_{s, a}$
- **Meaning:** A student is marked as positive ($Y_s = 1$) if they score $< 50$ on **any** assessment submitted during their course enrollment.
- **Empirical Base Rate:**
  - Snapshot-level at-risk prevalence: **8.56%** ($29,246 / 341,748$).
  - Student-level positive prevalence: **33.49%** ($7,821 / 23,351$).
- **Impact:** Because students attempt an average of **7.32 assessments** (range: 1 to 27), multi-opportunity probability compounding inflates the positive pool nearly fourfold.

### 2.2 Verification 2: Mathematical Denominators for Precision@k and Recall@k
The audit inspected the exact implementation in `src/model_optimization.py`:
$$\text{Recall@}k = \frac{\sum_{i \in \text{Top-}k} Y_i}{N_{\text{pos}}}, \quad \text{Precision@}k = \frac{\sum_{i \in \text{Top-}k} Y_i}{m}$$
Where:
- $N = 23,351$ total unique students.
- $N_{\text{pos}} = 7,821$ positive students (denominator for Recall).
- $m = \lceil k \times N \rceil$ (denominator for Precision; e.g. for $k=0.10$, $m = 2,336$).
- The mathematical calculation is technically correct relative to the defined variables, but its clinical interpretation depends on the validity of $P_s$ and $Y_s$.

### 2.3 Verification 3: Root Cause of the 96.27% Precision at Top 10%
In Phase 6, Precision reached **96.27%** (2,248 true positives out of 2,336 students in top 10%). The audit identified three compounding factors:
1. **High Base Rate (33.49%):** 1 in 3 students in the dataset failed at least one assessment.
2. **Extreme Timing Distortion in Top 10%:**
   - For the 2,336 students ranked in the top 10%, the mean scheduled date of the assessment generating their peak risk was **Day 155.2** (median Day 158.0; range: Day 19 to 261).
   - Only **33 students (1.4%)** had their peak risk in the first month (Day 0–30).
   - **1,749 students (74.87%)** had their peak risk on assessments scheduled **after Day 120**.
3. **Mismatched Assessment Credit:**
   - Of the 2,248 students credited as true positives, **only 1,465 (62.71% of the top 10%) actually failed the specific assessment that generated their peak probability**.
   - The remaining **783 students (33.5% of the top 10%) passed the peak-risk assessment**, but were counted as true positives because they happened to fail an entirely different assessment earlier or later in the academic year.

### 2.4 Verification 4: Comparability of Prediction Horizons
- **Snapshot Evaluation:** Evaluates a strictly fixed, prospectively valid 7-day lead time ($T = D_k - 7$) before each assessment.
- **Student Peak-Risk Evaluation:** Pools lead times and decision points spanning **February to October (9 months)** into a single static ranking.
- **Conclusion:** **Snapshot and student-level results do NOT use comparable prediction horizons.** An advisor at semester launch cannot act on predictions generated 5 months into the future.

### 2.5 Verification 5: Comparability of Target Prevalence in Chronological Evaluation
- **Snapshot Level:**
  - Grouped Cross-Validation: **8.56%** at-risk rate.
  - Chronological Test (`2014J` all students): **9.27%** at-risk rate.
  - Chronological Test (`2014J` novel students): **8.56%** at-risk rate.
  - *Prevalences are highly comparable.*
- **Student Level (Retrospective Max):**
  - Grouped Cross-Validation: **33.49%** ($7,821 / 23,351$).
  - Chronological Test (`2014J` all students): **33.30%** ($2,866 / 8,607$).
  - *Retrospective prevalences are also comparable.*

### 2.6 Verification 6: Reproducibility of Intervention Rankings
The audit verified that all prediction snapshots contain: `id_student`, `id_assessment`, `code_module`, `code_presentation`, `scheduled_date`, `at_risk`, and `prob_optimized_hgb`.
- **Conclusion:** **100% reproducible.** Any prospective or retrospective ranking strategy can be deterministically reproduced directly from `data/processed/oof_predictions_optimized_14day_delay.csv` without re-training models.

## 3. Strictly Leakage-Free Student-Level Alternatives

To provide educational institutions with scientifically defensible, prospectively actionable decision lists, two leakage-free alternatives were implemented and benchmarked:

### Alternative A: First-Assessment Cohort Milestone (Assessment 1 per Student)
- **Operational Logic:** The first scheduled assessment (TMA 1 / CMA 1) is the critical formative checkpoint that determines early dropout momentum.
- **Sampling:** Every student evaluated strictly 7 days before their first scheduled assignment ($N=23,351$ unique students; strictly 1 observation per student).
- **Target:** $at\_risk = 1$ if score on Assessment 1 is $< 50$ (Base failure rate: **10.76%**, $N=2,513$).

### Alternative B: Common Calendar Decision Cutoff (Day 30 Outreach Campaign)
- **Operational Logic:** University advisors conduct an institution-wide outreach campaign at a synchronized calendar milestone: **Day 30 of the semester**.
- **Sampling:** For all active students, identify their next immediately upcoming scheduled assessment ($D_k > 30$). Rank students using only the prediction available at or before Day 30 ($N=21,719$ active students; strictly 1 observation per student).
- **Target:** $at\_risk = 1$ if score on that upcoming assessment is $< 50$ (Base failure rate: **7.72%**, $N=1,676$).

## 7. Actionable Recommendations for System Architecture

1. **Retire Peak-Risk Retrospective Aggregation for Operational Use:**
   Peak-risk aggregation ($P_s = \max_a \hat{p}_{s, a}$) should only be cited as a retrospective diagnostic summary of total academic exposure, never as an advisor intervention roster.
2. **Adopt Common Calendar Milestone Intervention (Day 30):**
   Advisors should operate on synchronized calendar checkpoints (e.g. Day 30). At this point, every student has an upcoming target assessment, exactly one prediction, a realistic base failure rate (7.72%), and a top 20% outreach budget captures **59.37% of all students who will fail**.
3. **Preserve Separation of Artifacts:**
   Phase 5 and Phase 6 historical artifacts remain completely untouched. All corrected prospective metrics are maintained separately in `reports/intervention_evaluation_corrected_summary.json` and `reports/intervention_evaluation_audit.md`.
